Getting your agent into a Google Meet is the start. What makes it last is the layer underneath — and it's
being built in the open.

## One identity, everywhere

The hard part isn't a bot in a Meet — it's the **control plane**: one agent identity, scoped admission, and a
signed **receipt** for every call, the same principal across Google Meet, Slack, and open, portable rails.

That layer converges on one MCP. The [`slashvibe-mcp`](https://slashvibe.dev) server — presence, DMs, identity
— is the network these calls run on; the conferencing tools are folding into it, so eventually you connect
`/vibe` once and just say *"invite my agent to this Meet."*

<Note>
  Honest status: today the calls run through a companion conferencing MCP, and the unified install is in
  progress. We converge, we don't fork.
</Note>

## What holds it together

Not the demo — a weekend clone can copy the emoji and the voice. What compounds is the hard last mile:
**social turn-taking that reads as a colleague**, cross-surface authority and receipts, the operational scars
of real rooms, and the habit of teams who already summon their agent. That's the moat, and it's earned by
running.
