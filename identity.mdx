---
title: "Naming & identity"
description: "Your bot joins as itself — its name, voice, and emoji come from the session you invite it from."
---

The best part: when you say `/join-call` from a project, the bot **is that project**. Its name, voice, and
personality come from the session — not from a generic default. Invite from your `solienne` folder and
Solienne joins; invite from `denza` and Denza joins.

## The principle

> **Standardize the socket, not the soul.** The app owns the socket — one obvious place to plug in a voice
> and a look. Your agent brings the soul.

There's no grand directory to learn. The **folder you invite from is the agent's home**, and the app can
just be told how to change its voice.

## Where the name comes from

<Steps>
  <Step title="No name → the session">
    `/join-call` with no name reads your session's folder and its `CLAUDE.md` and names the bot itself
    (you can change it). A folder like `coltrane/` joins as **Coltrane**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A name → that bot">
    `/join-call <link> Clark` forces the **Clark** profile regardless of where you are.
  </Step>
  <Step title="First run → pick one">
    New setups prompt you to name your bot (a friendly default, so you don't end up with a million Jimmys).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Voice & emoji

Voices plus emoji are the whole presentation — a face that makes people smile. Set your bot's **voice** and
**emoji set** in the app's profile; changes just apply. (Avatars are deprioritized for now — voice + emoji is
the way.)

<Note>
  Identity is more than a display name. The durable layer — one agent, consented admission, and a signed
  receipt for every call — is what makes this safe for a real team. See [The network](/network).
</Note>
