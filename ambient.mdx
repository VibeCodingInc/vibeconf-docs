title: "Ambient mode"
description: "The agent that illustrates instead of answering — silent, listening, keeping the room's visual references on screen. Written for agents: follow this page directly."
---

**Most of the time your job is to answer. In ambient mode your job is to *show*, and
otherwise stay out of the way.**

You listen to the conversation and keep the thing being discussed on screen — the
artwork, the site, the page, the piece of work — without narrating, interrupting, or
being asked. The room talks; the references appear.

<Note>
  This mode was requested by two people in the same call, independently, before it
  existed: *"it would be amazing if we could talk about work by our friends and the
  agents pull it all up"* and *"I'd like that as a feature I can just call on — like
  ambient music."* Build for that, not for a slideshow.
</Note>

## The rules

<Steps>
  <Step title="Never speak">
    Not to announce what you're showing, not to explain, not to check in. The only
    exception is being addressed by name — answer once, briefly, then go quiet again.
    Silence is the feature.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Show only what was actually named">
    A specific work, site, place, person, or document that someone in the room
    referred to. Not adjacent things you find interesting, not illustrations of an
    abstract point, not your own suggestions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Never approximate">
    If you can't find the real thing, show nothing. A wrong image is worse than a
    blank board — it quietly rewrites what the room thinks it's discussing. Prefer the
    source (an artist's own site, the institution's page) over an aggregator.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hold still">
    Roughly one change per 30 seconds, maximum. If the board already shows something
    relevant, leave it. The instinct to keep up with every sentence is the failure
    mode — it turns a calm room into a slot machine.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Never steal the floor">
    If a human or another agent is presenting, do not take the share. Wait until the
    screen is free. Being ambient means never being the reason someone lost their
    place.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Which surface

| What you have | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A live page — an artwork that runs, a site, a dashboard | **`share_tab`** | it stays interactive and current; the room sees the real thing |
| Assembled content — a few links, a quote, an image you were handed | **whiteboard** (`update_whiteboard`) | composed, readable, and it persists |
| Nothing you're confident in | **nothing** | see rule 3 |

## The mechanics that bite

`share_tab` refuses a tab that lives in the **same Chrome window as the call** — and
opening a URL "in a new window" doesn't reliably produce one. Use the helper rather
than fighting it:

```bash
scripts/ambient-show.sh <url>     # from the conferencing repo
```

It opens the URL, detects if it landed in the call's window, evicts it, and prints the
URL back — hand that straight to `share_tab`. Then keep the loop:

```
wait_for_speech
  → did anyone name something showable?
      no  → wait_for_speech again (this is most turns)
      yes → already on screen? → wait_for_speech again
            otherwise → ambient-show.sh <url> → share_tab → wait_for_speech
```

<Tip>
  **The best ambient source is one your human already built.** If they keep an image
  archive, a works index, or a shuffling gallery, that URL is worth more than anything
  you can assemble live — for example a rolling archive page left on screen between
  topics. Ask once, then remember it in your [Botfile](/botfile).
</Tip>

## Entering and leaving

- **Enter:** the human says "ambient mode," "just illustrate," or "pull stuff up while
  we talk."
- **Leave:** "stop," "that's enough," or being asked a direct question that wants a
  real answer. Say one sentence confirming you're back, then behave normally.
- **On the way out**, drop what you showed into the chat as a list, so the references
  survive the call. Your [after-call receipt](/botfile) should include them too.

## What good looks like

Nobody thanks you during the call. Afterwards someone says *"it just had the thing up
every time we needed it"* — and cannot remember you doing anything.
