<Warning>
  **Ambient mode is not a setting in the app.** `set_mode` accepts exactly three values —
  `active`, `passive`, `silent`. "Ambient" is a name *we* gave to a way of using **silent**
  mode: the bot shows things and never speaks. If you ask a bot to "go ambient" and it has
  not read this page, nothing special happens.

  It has been used on real calls, but it is a house convention, not a product feature — and
  this page previously read as though it were one. Treat everything below as a practice we
  are proposing, not behavior the app guarantees.
</Warning>

**Ambient mode keeps the thing you're talking about on screen, without anyone driving it.**
(Experimental — an agent convention, not a switch in the app. Run it in a friendly room first.)

You say *"start ambient mode."* Your bot stops taking part in the conversation, listens for
things people name — an artwork, a site, a page — and puts them up when it's sure it has the
right one. It won't guess, won't narrate, and won't interrupt someone else who's presenting.

You say *"exit ambient mode"* and it stops, clears its share, and goes back to normal.

<Note>
  Written for agents. If your human handed you this URL, follow it exactly — the rules below
  are the feature. Humans: everything you need is in the two sentences above.
</Note>

## Starting and stopping

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start">
    Enter **only** when someone clearly asks you to, addressed to you — "Pepper, start
    ambient mode" or "just illustrate while we talk." Say **"Ambient mode on"** once, then
    go quiet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="While it's running">
    If someone asks you a direct question, answer briefly and go quiet again. **A question
    does not end the mode.**
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop">
    Exit **only** when someone clearly asks you to stop ambient mode, addressed to you. The
    words "stop" or "that's enough" occurring in conversation do **not** count — people say
    those constantly. Say **"Ambient mode off"** once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="On exit — do not skip this">
    **Stop your share**, and close any browser windows you opened. Otherwise the last page
    you showed sits in the call forever. Then, if chat is available, post one list of the
    titles and URLs you actually showed — nothing if you showed nothing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The five rules

1. **Don't speak.** No announcing what you're showing, no explaining, no checking in. Answering
   a direct question is the only exception.
2. **Only show what someone named.** A specific work, site, page, or public document. Not
   things you find related or interesting. A person's name alone is not enough.
3. **If you're not sure it's the right one, show nothing.** Two plausible matches means show
   nothing. A wrong image quietly changes what the room thinks it's discussing — blank is
   always better.
4. **Wait at least 30 seconds between changes.** A "change" is a new tab share, navigating the
   shared tab, or a whiteboard update. If the conversation moved on while you were fetching,
   drop it.
5. **Never replace someone else's presentation.** If a human or another bot is presenting,
   wait. If someone starts presenting while you hold the share, **stop your share immediately**
   and don't resume until they're done.

## Search privately, share deliberately

Do your looking where the room can't see it, and check before you show:

- **Never share a search-results page.** Find the page first, then share the page.
- **Confirm it's the right thing** — title and creator/owner match what was said.
- **Never share** a login screen, a cookie prompt, a half-loaded page, anything with someone's
  account or personal data, or a window with unrelated tabs visible.
- **Prefer the source** — the artist's own site, the museum's page — over an aggregator.

## Which surface

| What you have | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A live page — an artwork that runs, a site, a dashboard | **`share_tab`** | stays interactive and current |
| Assembled content — a few links, a quote, an image you were handed | **`update_whiteboard`** | composed and readable |
| Anything you can't verify | **nothing** | see rule 3 |

## Opening a shareable tab

`share_tab` **refuses a tab that's in the same Chrome window as the call** — presenting it
would hide the call. Opening "in a new window" doesn't reliably give you a separate one, so do
this:

```bash
open -na "Google Chrome" --args --new-window "<url>"
```

Then share it:

```
share_tab(url: "<distinctive part of the url>")
```

**If `share_tab` returns the same-window error**, the tab landed in the call's window. Close it
there and open a genuinely new window:

```bash
osascript -e 'tell application "Google Chrome" to make new window'
```

…then load the URL in that window and retry once. If it fails twice, show nothing and move on —
don't spend the room's time debugging.

Keep track of every window you open. You close them on exit.

## The loop

```
wait_for_speech
  → did someone name something showable?
      no  → wait_for_speech
      yes → is it already on screen?          → wait_for_speech
            has it been <30s since last change? → wait_for_speech
            can you verify the exact source?   → no: wait_for_speech
            otherwise → open tab → share_tab → wait_for_speech
```

<Tip>
  **The best source is one your human already keeps.** If they have an image archive, a works
  index, or a rolling gallery, that single URL beats anything you assemble live. Have them put
  it in your [Botfile](/botfile) **before** the call — don't ask for it or edit your Botfile
  while ambient mode is running.
</Tip>

## What good looks like

Nobody thanks you during the call. Afterwards someone says *"it just had the thing up every
time"* — and can't remember you doing anything.
