---
title: "Bringing your bot to someone else's meeting"
description: "The admission mechanics and the social choreography for a call you don't host — written from a real transcript."
---

Hosting your own call with a bot is easy: you admit it, you set the tone. Bringing your
bot to **someone else's** meeting is a different game — the admission controls belong to
the host, and the "wait, who's joining?" moment lands on you. This page is the
choreography, written from a real call where a beta user negotiated it live (it worked).

## Before the call

- **Tell the host a bot is coming.** One line in the invite or the chat beforehand:
  *"Heads up — I'm bringing my AI notetaker/whiteboard bot, it'll knock at the start."*
  A surprise knock from an unfamiliar name is how bots get denied.
- **One bot, ideally.** A second bot doubles the admission friction and Meet sometimes
  balks at admitting it at all. Bring the one that has a job to do.

## Getting admitted

- **The host must admit it — you can't.** Your bot shows up in the waiting queue like any
  guest. Expect a little "do you see it? I think *you* have to admit them" shuffle; that's
  normal today.
- **Meet may flag it as a "potential risk"** with no visible Admit button. The Admit is
  behind the **⋮ overflow menu** on the flagged entry — tell the host that sentence
  before they conclude it's impossible. (Full detail in
  [troubleshooting](/troubleshooting).)
- **If a second bot won't admit,** drop it rather than derailing the meeting's first two
  minutes. You can always bring it next time.

## The introduction — this is the part that matters

Don't let the bot be an unexplained tile. The move that works, verbatim from the field:

> "So everyone, please meet Taylor Script. I'm testing a bot that live-whiteboards with
> us in meetings. Taylor, would you mind starting a whiteboard and taking some notes
> during this call?"

Three beats: **name it → say what it's for → give it a visible job.** Once the room has
seen it do the job, the weirdness evaporates and the meeting proceeds. People mostly
find it fun — one host's reaction on record: "that was funny, I especially like the name."

## During the call

- **Guest bots stay quiet.** In a room of strangers your bot should be in *passive* or
  *silent* mode — visible work (whiteboard, notes), no unsolicited voice. Say "just
  listen and take notes" before the call starts.
- **It can coexist with recorders.** Running alongside an Otter/Granola-style notetaker
  is fine and actually clarifying: the recorder captures, your bot *makes things*.
- **You own its behavior.** If it talks over someone or shares at the wrong moment,
  mute it or tell it to stop — same as you'd manage a guest you brought.

## After

The receipt/summary your bot produces is a guest gift: share it with the host rather
than assuming they want it published. Their meeting, their call.
