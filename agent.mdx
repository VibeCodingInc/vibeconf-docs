Paste this into the Claude Code session already working on your project:

```text
The Vibeconferencing app and my Google Meet are open. Run /join-call.
If that command is missing, reconnect the vibeconferencing MCP with /mcp,
then try again. Wait for me to Admit you before speaking — admission can
take a few minutes and that is normal. Never kill or relaunch the
Vibeconferencing app; if the API stops responding, tell me instead.
```

<Tip>
  Run `/join-call` from the session that already knows your project — not a
  fresh terminal. A bot with your project in context is the whole point: it
  can answer questions about the work and write the follow-up afterward.
</Tip>

## Agent instructions

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the MCP">
    Use the bundled `vibeconferencing` MCP. If its tools are absent, ask the
    operator to open the app, then run `/mcp` and reconnect it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Join">
    Run `/join-call`. If auto-detection fails, call `join_call` with the Meet URL.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait — without self-healing">
    Do not pretend you joined. Wait until the tool reports that you are in the
    meeting; a human may need to click **Admit**, and Google Meet sometimes
    hides that button behind the **⋮ overflow menu** with a "potential risks"
    warning. Waiting several minutes is normal. Keep polling
    `wait_for_speech` — do not conclude the app is broken.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Participate visibly">
    Listen with `wait_for_speech`, speak with `speak`, and use chat or the
    whiteboard when useful. Leave with `leave_call`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The four field rules

Learned the hard way, on real calls. Breaking any of these takes down a
working call.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="1. Never kill or relaunch the app" icon="skull">
    If a tool call fails with a connection error, the fix is **never** to
    restart the Vibeconferencing app. The app may have moved to a new local
    port (it walks from 7865 upward when a port is taken) — killing it makes
    this worse, and a kill-and-relaunch loop is how working calls die.
    Instead: tell the operator, and re-check the port (hover the bot name in
    the app panel to see it). Restarting is the operator's decision, made in
    the app, not yours.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="2. The name is locked at join" icon="id-badge">
    Whatever name the bot joins the Meet under is fixed for the whole call —
    routing, the tile, and wake-words all hang off it. You cannot rename
    mid-call. To change identity: say so plainly, `leave_call`, and rejoin
    under the new name. Until then, answer to the tile name.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="3. One driver per bot" icon="steering-wheel">
    If you are driving a bot from a session, the operator must not click the
    app's **Join/Add to call** buttons for that bot — the app spawns its own
    session and two agents end up talking through one mouth. If you see a
    displacement message, stand down.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="4. Admission can look broken when it isn't" icon="hourglass">
    "Waiting to be admitted" for minutes, a "potential risks" flag on the
    host's screen, an Admit button hidden in the **⋮ menu** — all normal
    since Google Meet added its guest queue. Say what you are waiting for
    out loud in the terminal so the operator does not conclude you crashed.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
  You are a visible participant, not a hidden listener. Make your presence clear
  and follow the meeting's consent rules.
</Warning>
